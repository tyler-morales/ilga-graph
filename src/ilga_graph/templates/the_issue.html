The issue

Illinois is blocking registration for federally lawful Kei (軽) vehicles due to a statutory interpretation gap. Here’s what’s happening and how a narrow fix can restore registration without weakening safety or insurance rules.

What’s happening

Registration is being denied or revoked for Kei-class vehicles that are legally imported and federally compliant. Illinois is applying 625 ILCS 5/3-401(c-1) to treat these vehicles as “non-highway” and therefore ineligible for normal registration, even though they are street-legal in other states and meet federal standards.

Why it matters

This is a statutory clarification issue, not a paperwork or compliance problem. The law was not written with Kei vehicles in mind; the current interpretation creates a gap that blocks lawful, safe, and already-common vehicles (including trucks and vans used for delivery, groundskeeping, and small business) from being registered in Illinois.

The fix

A narrow clarification amendment can restore normal registration for these vehicles while keeping all existing safety, equipment, and insurance requirements intact. The goal is to align Illinois’s treatment of federally compliant Kei vehicles with the intent of the law and with how other states handle them.
