Illinois Kei Vehicle Registration: A narrow statutory fix to clarify 625 ILCS 5/3-401(c-1). Two-page briefing for Illinois legislators and staff (as of 2026-02-19).

{% endblock %} {% block brief_main_heading %}Official brief{% endblock %} {% block brief_main %}

Issue in one sentence

Illinois is treating Kei vehicles that are lawfully imported under federal rules as off-highway/non-highway based on an interpretation of 625 ILCS 5/3-401(c-1)—blocking normal titling and registration for road use.

Core ambiguity: SOS reads “manufactured for operation on highways” as “manufactured for U.S. highways.”

What the Secretary of State is relying on (current posture)

The Secretary of State has pointed to 625 ILCS 5/3-401(c-1), which says a vehicle may not be registered unless it: 1) was originally manufactured for operation on highways; 2) is a modification of a vehicle originally manufactured for operation on highways; or 3) was assembled from component parts designed for highway vehicles.

We are not disputing SOS’s authority to enforce the Vehicle Code; we are asking the General Assembly to clarify how highway-built imported vehicles fit within it.

Key takeaway: Under the SOS’s own framing, the barrier is not a paperwork glitch—it’s a statutory clarity gap. In practice, states that allow ongoing registration generally do so through explicit statutory authorization and/or documented agency policy.

Why a narrowly tailored statute is the most direct fix

Illinois can keep existing safety and equipment enforcement intact while clarifying that a foreign-manufactured vehicle originally designed for highway use (in another country) can be titled/registered here when it is lawfully imported under federal rules (often via the 25-year NHTSA exemption; emissions requirements still apply).

Proposed legislative concept (drafting direction)

Add a targeted clarification to 625 ILCS 5/3-401(c-1) (or a related definitional section) so that a vehicle may be registered if it was originally manufactured for operation on highways in any jurisdiction and is legally importable into the United States under federal law (commonly 25+ years). Eligibility would still require normal Illinois titling documentation, insurance, and compliance with Illinois equipment and traffic laws.

Policy posture: We are asking for normal registration for highway-built Kei vehicles. If operating limits are desired, they should be narrowly tailored and evidence-based—not achieved by categorizing all highway-built Kei vehicles as off-highway.

What we are asking your office to do

  1. Help identify the best legislative vehicle and sponsor path (Transportation / Vehicle Code).
  2. Facilitate a staff-level meeting with SOS legal/policy to confirm what statutory language would satisfy their interpretation.
  3. If supportive, commit to carry or co-sponsor a narrowly drafted fix in the next filing window.
