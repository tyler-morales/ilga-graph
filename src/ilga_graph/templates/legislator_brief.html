Kei vehicle registration in Illinois — statutory gap and proposed clarification.

{% endblock %} {% block brief_main_heading %}Executive summary{% endblock %} {% block brief_main %}

Illinois is currently denying or revoking registration for federally lawful Kei (軽) class vehicles—small, street-legal trucks and vans—by applying 625 ILCS 5/3-401(c-1) in a way that treats them as non-highway. This is a statutory interpretation gap, not a safety or compliance issue. A narrow clarification amendment can restore normal registration while leaving safety, equipment, and insurance requirements unchanged.

Current law

625 ILCS 5/3-401(c-1) governs when certain vehicles may be registered. The provision is being applied to treat Kei vehicles as ineligible for normal highway registration, despite their federal compliance and use on public roads in other states. The statute was not written with this vehicle class in mind, leading to inconsistent interpretation and blocked registration for lawful owners.

Impact

Registration is being denied or revoked for vehicles that are legally imported, meet federal standards, and are commonly used for delivery, groundskeeping, agriculture, and small business. Owners who have complied with federal and state rules are unable to register their vehicles in Illinois, while the same vehicles remain street-legal elsewhere.

Proposed solution

A narrow clarification amendment that explicitly allows registration of federally compliant Kei-class vehicles under existing safety, equipment, and insurance requirements. No weakening of those requirements is proposed—only clarification so that vehicles that are lawful and safe under federal and other state frameworks can be registered in Illinois.

Key facts

  • Safety. Kei vehicles are built to regulated standards and are used on public roads in other states and in highly regulated jurisdictions with strong safety records.
  • Federal compliance. Vehicles at issue are 25+ years old and legally imported under federal rules; they are not off-road or unregulated equipment.
  • Other states. Many states allow registration of these vehicles without similar statutory barriers; Illinois's interpretation is an outlier.
  • Use cases. Common uses include small-business delivery, university and park groundskeeping, and agricultural support—applications where size and efficiency are advantages.
