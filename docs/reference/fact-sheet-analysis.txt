Analysis of Sample Fact Sheets
================================================================================

1. National Partnership for Women & Families – "Gun Violence: A Threat to Women and Families" (2018)

This three‑page fact sheet addresses how gun violence affects women and families.  It opens with a headline and a brief paragraph describing gun violence as a public‑health crisis and summarising the number of women killed by intimate partners.  The document uses subheadings and bullet lists to organise statistics:

	•	Scale of the problem: It notes that guns kill almost 30 000 people annually and injure another 60 000.  It highlights that more than half of mass shootings are related to domestic or family violence.
	•	Effects on women and children: Women are five times more likely to be killed when a gun is present in an intimate‑partner violence situation; 4.5 million women have been threatened with a gun.  Children account for 25 % of mass‑shooting victims and 16 % of younger children's firearm deaths occur when guns are mistaken for toys.
	•	Disproportionate impact on communities of colour: Statistics show that Black, Latino and American Indian children are more likely to be killed by firearms than white children.
	•	Policy recommendations: The final section urges policymakers to treat gun violence as a public‑health emergency and calls for comprehensive policies such as expanded background checks and gun buy‑back programs.

Design strengths:
	•	Uses a clear heading and an opening summary to hook readers.
	•	Bullet lists with bolded numbers make statistics easy to scan.
	•	Sections are labeled ("Gun violence threatens women's health," "Gun violence is a leading cause of children's deaths," etc.).
	•	Concludes with specific policy actions, fulfilling the call‑to‑action requirement.

Take‑aways for kei‑vehicle advocacy: This fact sheet demonstrates how to connect statistics to personal safety and public‑health framing.  For a kei‑vehicle fact sheet, a similar approach could highlight fatality rates of larger vehicles vs. kei vehicles, emphasise the safety record, and call for supportive legislation.

2. CT Health Policy Project – Sample Fact Sheet on Connecticut Medicaid

This one‑page fact sheet summarises Connecticut's Medicaid program.  It starts with a short introduction explaining that Medicaid is Connecticut's largest coverage program.  The document highlights key numbers in a "Numbers at a Glance" section: total Medicaid enrollment, percentage of state population covered, newly eligible members under the Affordable Care Act, and total Medicaid spending.  A small chart compares state and federal funding shares.

Design strengths:
	•	Uses a one‑page layout with plenty of white space.
	•	A "Numbers at a Glance" box allows busy readers to grasp the scale of the program quickly.
	•	A simple bar chart reinforces the 58.6 % federal vs. 41.4 % state funding split.
	•	The introduction and headings provide context without overwhelming detail.

Take‑aways: For an advocacy fact sheet on kei vehicles, a "numbers at a glance" section could summarise benefits (fuel economy, cost savings, emissions reductions) and adoption statistics.  Simple charts comparing kei vehicle emissions to those of standard vehicles would reinforce the message.

3. Feed My Starving Children (FMSC) – Organizational Fact Sheet (2016)

FMSC is a Christian non‑profit that provides nutrition to malnourished people.  Its fact sheet opens with "Who is FMSC?" and summarises the organisation's mission and history.  It then breaks into sections:
	•	Our food: Describes three fortified meal formulas and their ingredients.
	•	Costs: States that each meal costs less than $0.25 to produce.
	•	Volunteers: Notes that more than one million volunteers packaged 273 million meals in 2015.
	•	Distribution partners: Lists the types of organisations that distribute meals.
	•	Donors & funding: Emphasises that FMSC is 100 % donor‑funded and devotes more than 90 % of donations directly to feeding kids.
	•	Locations: Provides a short list of packing sites and contact information.

The fact sheet includes an inspirational tagline and scriptural quote and ends with contact details and website.

Design strengths:
	•	Clearly segmented sections with bold headings.
	•	Use of specific numbers (cost per meal, number of volunteers) to illustrate impact.
	•	Includes organisational mission, program details, volunteer opportunities and donation appeal in a compact format.

Take‑aways: A kei‑vehicle fact sheet could adapt this structure by including sections on the organisation ("Who we are"), benefits of kei vehicles (safety, environmental, affordability), how supporters can volunteer or advocate, and donation/support information.

4. No Kid Hungry – "The Summer Hunger Solution" Fact Sheet (2019)

This single‑issue fact sheet addresses hunger during summer when school meals are unavailable.  It frames the problem in its opening paragraphs and explains that the USDA's summer meals program provides a solution.  The sheet then highlights:
	•	Barriers: Lack of public awareness; many families do not know about summer meal sites.
	•	Texting program: Describes the No Kid Hungry texting program that connects families to nearby meal sites by texting "FOOD" or "COMIDA" to 877‑877.
	•	Compelling statistics: Lists the extra monthly grocery cost low‑income families face, and that 69 % of low‑income families must choose between food and medical care in summer.  It also notes that six out of seven hungry kids don't get the meals they need.
	•	Bottom line: Emphasises the benefits of feeding children—better health, learning and long‑term economic impact—and calls on various sectors to coordinate efforts.
	•	About section: Briefly describes the No Kid Hungry campaign and invites readers to join.

Design strengths:
	•	Presents a problem, solution and call to action in clear sections.
	•	Uses large statistics and a text‑message call‑to‑action that readers can easily act on.
	•	Ends with an empowering "bottom line" summary.

Take‑aways: A kei‑vehicle advocacy fact sheet could replicate this formula: clearly identify the problem (e.g., lack of legal recognition or safety misconceptions), describe a solution (policy changes, incentives), provide simple ways for readers to act (join mailing list, contact legislator, sign a petition) and end with a motivating summary.

5. Mothers Against Drunk Driving (MADD) – Drunk Driving Statistics Web Page

Although not a downloadable PDF, MADD's "Drunk Driving Statistics" page functions like a fact sheet.  Each statistic is presented as a headline: for example, in 2017, 10 874 people died in drunk‑driving crashes—"one every 48 minutes".  Additional call‑out stats include:
	•	Almost 7 % of drivers involved in fatal crashes tested positive for THC, while alcohol levels above the legal limit were found in 21 % of those drivers.
	•	Drugs other than alcohol are involved in about 18 % of driver deaths, and 22 % of drivers tested positive for illegal or prescription drugs.
	•	4 300 people under 21 were killed due to drunk driving.
	•	MADD lists its impact: more than 902 750 victims served as of December 2021, ignition‑interlock laws passed in 34 states and DC, and a supportive service is provided every four minutes.

Each statistic is accompanied by citations and a "View Citation" link, and there is a donation call to action at the end.

Design strengths:
	•	Uses bold headlines and short paragraphs for each statistic—readers can skim and absorb key facts quickly.
	•	Provides citations to credible sources (NHTSA, MADD reports).
	•	Includes a donation appeal and contact information.

Take‑aways: Even if you cannot design a PDF, a web‑based fact sheet can still be effective.  For kei‑vehicle advocacy, a web page could list key safety and economic benefits with citations and provide an easy way for visitors to join or donate.

6. Environmental Justice Factsheet – University of Michigan Center for Sustainable Systems (2025)

This multi‑page fact sheet defines environmental justice and provides detailed bullet lists across categories such as built environment, food, energy and materials.  It explains that environmental justice means the just treatment and meaningful involvement of all people in environmental decisions.  Each category contains data points; for example:
	•	People of colour make up 42 % of the US population but 57 % of those living in counties with unhealthy air.
	•	Drinking water contaminated with PFAS affects Hispanic/Latino populations at 1.5–2 times the rate of communities without PFAS.
	•	In 2010, 45 % of those living within two miles of toxic waste facilities were people of colour.
	•	Food insecurity affected 13.5 % of US households in 2023 and was consistently higher among Black and Hispanic households.

The fact sheet continues with sections on energy, hydropower and dams, energy poverty, materials, mining and electronic waste, each with bullet points and references.  Sources are cited and a recommended citation is provided at the end.

Design strengths:
	•	Comprehensive yet well‑organised, allowing readers to focus on specific subtopics.
	•	Uses bullet points for quick reading and includes data for context.
	•	Provides citations and a suggested citation format, enhancing credibility.

Take‑aways: When an advocacy topic has multiple dimensions (e.g., social equity, environmental impact), dividing information into thematic sections can help readers navigate.  A kei‑vehicle fact sheet might include sections on environmental benefits, economic benefits, safety record, regulatory challenges and international examples.

7. American Heart Association – "2026 Heart Disease and Stroke Statistics Update: At‑a‑Glance Fact Sheet"

This fact sheet summarises key US and global statistics related to cardiovascular diseases (CVD).  It opens with a statement that the document contains "key statistics about heart disease, stroke, other cardiovascular diseases and their risk factors".  Bullet lists highlight:
	•	The Heart Association is the largest non‑governmental funder of cardiovascular research and has invested over $6.1 billion since 1949.
	•	CVD accounted for 915 973 deaths in the US in 2023, and 130.6 million US adults (48.9 %) had some form of CVD.
	•	The age‑adjusted mortality rate for coronary heart disease and stroke, cost data and global death figures.

Each statistic includes a bullet symbol, and the sheet has separate subheadings for coronary heart disease and stroke.  The document emphasises that these data are drawn from a larger annual report and provides context about changes in methodology.

Design strengths:
	•	Presents complex epidemiological data in bullet form with plain language.
	•	Includes context about methodology and notes where data may differ from previous updates.
	•	Highlights the organisation's research role and funding impact.

Take‑aways: For kei‑vehicle advocacy, a similar at‑a‑glance sheet could summarise statistics such as number of kei vehicles on the road, emissions reductions compared to standard cars, crash data, and economic impact on owners.  Providing context about data sources and methodology increases credibility.

================================================================================
Recommendations for a Kei Vehicle Advocacy Fact Sheet
================================================================================

To design an effective fact sheet for kei‑vehicle advocacy, consider integrating the lessons from the fact sheets analysed above:

1.	Define the audience and main message. Identify whether the fact sheet targets policymakers (e.g., advocating for legal recognition of kei vehicles), potential supporters/donors or the general public.  Craft a clear title that summarises your goal—e.g., "Kei Vehicles: Affordable, Eco‑Friendly Mobility for Our Community".  Use the first paragraph to define kei vehicles and state why they matter (e.g., high fuel efficiency, low emissions, affordability).

2.	Use a skimmable structure. Organise the fact sheet into sections with concise headings, such as:
	•	What Are Kei Vehicles? Provide a brief definition and legal classification.
	•	Environmental & Safety Benefits: Highlight fuel economy, emissions reductions and safety records compared with larger vehicles; include key statistics in bullet form.
	•	Economic Impact: Summarise cost savings for owners (purchase price, maintenance, insurance) and potential economic benefits for local economies.
	•	International Examples: Present successful adoption and regulation of kei vehicles in Japan or other countries; use bullet points or small charts.
	•	Policy Recommendations / Call to Action: Describe specific actions readers can take—such as contacting legislators to support kei‑vehicle registration and tax incentives, joining a mailing list, donating or volunteering.

3.	Include compelling statistics and visuals. Use data from reliable sources (e.g., government transportation reports, environmental studies) to quantify benefits.  A "Numbers at a Glance" box could show average fuel economy (e.g., 60 mpg), typical CO₂ emissions reductions compared to standard cars, cost differences and number of kei vehicles operating in other countries.  Simple bar charts or infographics can enhance comprehension.

4.	Provide citations and further resources. At the bottom of the fact sheet, list sources for the data used (government reports, academic studies) and link to more detailed resources (full reports, your organisation's website).  This builds credibility and allows interested readers to learn more.

5.	Make the call to action prominent. Use bold or highlighted text to specify what you want readers to do—e.g., "Call your state representative and urge them to support legislation allowing kei vehicles on state roads," or "Sign up at [organization website] to receive updates and advocacy alerts."  Include any necessary details (contact information, bill numbers) so readers can act immediately.

6.	Keep it visually appealing and accessible. Use plenty of white space, avoid dense paragraphs, and aim for a reading level appropriate for a broad audience.  Include photographs of kei vehicles to make the issue tangible.  Ensure that charts and graphics are clearly labeled and colour‑blind friendly.

================================================================================
Conclusion
================================================================================

Effective fact sheets distil complex issues into clear, concise, and actionable documents.  Across the sample fact sheets analysed here, common strengths include brief introductions, organised sections with bullet lists, compelling statistics, visual elements, cited sources and a clear call to action.  Adapting these best practices to a kei‑vehicle advocacy fact sheet will help communicate the benefits of kei vehicles and mobilise readers to support your cause.
