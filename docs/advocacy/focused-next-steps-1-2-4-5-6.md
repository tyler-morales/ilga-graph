## 4. The 3–5 Conclusions We Want Every Legislator to Remember

*Source: Hardball Ch7 — "three to five conclusions from its argument can be presented in a clear, concise, and clever enough way to stick in legislators' consciousness." Content from STRATEGIC_FIVE_POINTS and the briefs.*

Use these everywhere: one-pager, scripts, fact sheet, verbal pitch.

1. **Kei vehicles are federally legal to import (25-year rule).** Illinois is blocking registration at the state level based on how it reads the statute, not because of federal law.

2. **21+ states already allow registration—Illinois is the outlier.** Other states have clarified statute or policy; we are asking for the same kind of narrow fix.

3. **The current Illinois statute has an ambiguity, not a prohibition.** SOS reads "manufactured for operation on highways" as "U.S. highways." The General Assembly can clarify that highway-built, federally lawful imports qualify.

4. **The fix is a narrow clarifying amendment—no new regulatory framework.** Safety, insurance, equipment, and traffic enforcement stay intact. We are not asking for exemptions from those.

5. **This affects real Illinois residents in your district.** Owners have had registrations denied or revoked and titles branded "Not Eligible for Registration." They are asking for a fix so they can register legal vehicles.

*Optional shorter "elevator" version (3 conclusions):* (1) Federally legal imports are being blocked by an Illinois interpretation, not federal law. (2) A narrow statutory clarification fixes it without weakening safety or enforcement. (3) Constituents in your district are affected and want a fix.

**The issue**  
Illinois is treating lawfully imported kei vehicles as off-highway, so owners cannot register them for normal road use. This is based on how Illinois interprets 625 ILCS 5/3-401(c-1), not on federal law or missing paperwork.

**Our position**  
We are asking for a narrow statutory clarification so that vehicles originally manufactured for highway use (in any jurisdiction) and lawfully importable under federal law may be titled and registered in Illinois under normal requirements (insurance, equipment, traffic laws). No weakening of safety or enforcement.
